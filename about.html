About Barbell Saves

Built by people who lived it. Guided by science. Powered by community.

Our Mission

We Built This Because the Old Way Wasn't Working

The Barbell Saves Outpatient Center grew out of The Barbell Saves Project — a Phoenix community organization helping Arizonans rebuild their lives through fitness and peer connection since 2014.

When relapse rates hovered between 40–60%, we knew the system needed something different — not a new form to fill out, but a complete rethinking of what recovery looks and feels like.

So we built it. A medically licensed outpatient center where movement is treatment, peer support is clinical, and every element is designed to create lasting change.

Our People

Led By Licensed Professionals

Our clinical team brings together licensed therapists, peer support specialists with lived experience, and medical staff — all working together under one roof toward one goal.

Every treatment plan is individually designed. Every session is clinically grounded. And every staff member is here because they believe this model of care changes lives.

What drives us

Our Core Values

🧠
Whole-Person Healing
Physical, mental, and social wellness are treated together — not in separate silos. Everything is connected, and our model reflects that.
🤝
Community First
Recovery happens in relationship. Our peer support specialists have lived experience — they're not observers, they're participants in the same journey.
💪
Movement as Medicine
Research shows exercise improves mood regulation, reduces cravings, and strengthens long-term recovery outcomes. We treat it as clinical, not optional.
🚫
Zero Judgment
Whether you're just starting or returning to care, our team meets you where you are. No shame, no barriers, just support.
📋
Evidence-Based
Every clinical intervention — DBT, CBT, trauma-informed care, somatic approaches — is grounded in research and delivered by licensed professionals.
🌎
Accessible to All
From AHCCCS Medicaid to Medicare to sliding scale self-pay, we've made sure finances are never the reason someone doesn't get help.
